How the curve is built

NPS = C − b·e−k(R − 80)

C is the ceiling you set. For each C, b and k are fitted by least squares to the 130 routes. The slope is b·k·e−k(R−80), which falls as R rises.

What the data can and can't tell you

Within the observed range (about 64% to 91%), ceilings from 55 to 100 fit almost equally well (see R²), and a straight line fits as well as any curve. The flattening above about 90% is an assumption, not something the data proves. Present it as a scenario.

Caveats for the report

NPS is simulated. The relationship is a correlation across routes, not proof that fixing punctuality causes NPS to rise. Routes differ in many other ways (trip purpose, complaints about staff, wifi, price). The typical route sits about ±10 NPS points off the curve.
